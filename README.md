# Mirza Husadzic

**Software engineer · AI systems architect · Independent researcher**

I build software and study how meaning can govern what we make with AI.

The question took form in a poem developed in dialogue with an AI system.
I gave models the poem as a conjecture and examined the worlds they made:
did its relations become structure and behavior, or did its words merely
reappear? I call this approach **semantic decomposition and recompilation**:
make the relations explicit, build from them, and test what they carry.

I keep my interpretation and added engineering choices open to inspection.
With the tools below, I ask what statements support, whether decisions depend
on their declared evidence, and who may approve or change the work. A test of
dependence establishes neither correctness nor permission to act.

Sustained human–AI collaboration is how I pursue this work. I want to recognize
my intention in something I could not make alone, while remaining able to
question and change what the work is for.

## Work

- [frame-loop](https://github.com/mirzahusadzic/claude-frame-loop): a semantic workflow linter for Claude Code; checks work against a person's confirmed requirements and uses Gadfly to test whether the reader's verdict depends on them. Early alpha. [![frame-loop release](https://img.shields.io/github/v/tag/mirzahusadzic/claude-frame-loop?sort=semver&label=frame-loop&logo=github&logoColor=white)](https://github.com/mirzahusadzic/claude-frame-loop/tags)
- [Gadfly](https://github.com/mirzahusadzic/gadfly): tests whether a decision rests on the evidence it is said to rest on. [![midwife on PyPI](https://img.shields.io/pypi/v/midwife?label=midwife&logo=pypi&logoColor=white)](https://pypi.org/project/midwife/)
- [Tendora](https://github.com/mirzahusadzic/tendora): answers questions from plain-language statements, names its sources and keeps what the statements do not settle open. A prototype with a public demo.
- [After the Poem](https://github.com/mirzahusadzic/after-the-poem): where this work began; studies how models turn a poem's conjecture into worlds, and which relations survive the transformation ([workbench](https://mirzahusadzic.github.io/after-the-poem/)).
- Underneath: [eGemma](https://github.com/mirzahusadzic/egemma) runs the models locally, and the [Unified Model Router](https://github.com/mirzahusadzic/unified-model-router) provides one API for hosted models with explicit session and privacy boundaries.

## Current explorations

- **Context and provenance:** How can the basis of an answer remain inspectable
  across sessions and handoffs, and what can still be claimed when its source
  trail is lost?
- **Meaning and inference:** How do questions, roles and assumptions change what
  can be inferred, and what must remain open? In Tendora, I explore this through
  Abstract Meaning Representation (AMR) and deterministic reasoning.
- **Authority across boundaries:** How can grants, scope, expiry, revocation and
  return obligations be made explicit when people, AI systems and tools delegate
  work? frame-loop and the router explore parts of this question.

## Background

I've worked across engineering simulation frameworks, enterprise communication
systems, automotive embedded software, distributed vehicle middleware and maritime
edge-to-cloud architecture. The common thread is understanding behavior under
real constraints, especially where responsibilities belong and what happens
when they are misplaced.

That experience shapes how I direct AI-assisted engineering: framing problems,
shaping context, guiding implementation, evaluating results and keeping
architectural intent tied to what ships.

If you're working on inspectable reasoning, agents that have to answer to a
person's own words, or systems that have to work outside the demo, I'm happy to
compare notes.
